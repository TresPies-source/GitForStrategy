# Liberation Psychology

*Remove the barrier, not the person.*

## What it is

Design structures that widen who can take part, and that re-center the voice of the people the work is for. Remove the structural barriers to contribution instead of asking people to overcome them.

## Why it matters

Tools and methods tend to concentrate capability in whoever built them. A method only its authors can practice is not yet a method. New structures that copy the old exclusions under a new name have changed nothing.

## The pattern

1. Name who cannot take part today, and why.
2. Find the structural barrier: jargon, cost, credentials, missing context.
3. Remove the barrier, not the person. Keep the standards.
4. Make the open path the default.
5. Judge by who can take part, not only by who does.

## Revisit when

You decide who a tool, document, or process is for. You write contribution rules. A review process starts rewarding insiders.

## What it refuses

Expertise gatekeeping. It also refuses removing all structure in the name of openness, which builds a different barrier: only the already-confident can find their way through chaos.

## Grew from

Liberation psychology as named by Ignacio Martín-Baró, who argued that psychology should start from the lived reality of oppressed majorities rather than from theories brought in by those in power. And Frantz Fanon's insistence that new structures must re-center human agency rather than reproduce the ones they replace.

### Sources

- Martín-Baró, Ignacio. *Writings for a Liberation Psychology*. Edited by Adrianne Aron and Shawn Corne. Harvard University Press, 1994.
- Fanon, Frantz. *The Wretched of the Earth*. Translated by Constance Farrington. Grove Press, 1963. First published in French in 1961.
